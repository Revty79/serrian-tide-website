# Worlds Bridge A: alternate historical timelines

## Existing architecture and extension

The approved starting release is `cb86d43a5aebf2ef89fa6533649a3e15dc77b163`; the published journal ends at 0111. Worlds owns private narrative records through `world.owner_id`. G.O.D./Administrator access and explicit read-only Administrator review are checked before reading a World. Owned-World transaction locks serialize authoring; record revisions protect independent drafts. The existing historical entries, eras, entry/era links, signed canonical World years, source reckonings and calendar notation remain the sources of truth. Atlas locations, settlement buildings, interior floors, dungeon sites and map connections remain stable World identities with archive/restore.

Bridge A extends these systems rather than adding another clock. A World has one persistent primary timeline, automatically created even for direct SQL World inserts. Existing historical records gain only an origin timeline reference. Their existing columns, identities, associations, precision, source notation and calendar references are preserved. New entries and eras first authored in alternatives receive ordinary World identities with that alternative as their immutable origin. Primary History queries use the primary origin, preventing branch accounts from appearing in primary history.

## Source versions and effective state

`world_timeline` stores permanent World/parent identities, canonical divergence year, authored name/description/explanation, revisions and lifecycle. Its primary row is permanent; ancestry and divergence cannot be changed after creation. Same-World composite foreign keys, a unique primary index, the World insert trigger and ancestry/lifecycle triggers enforce the important invariants. Requiring an existing parent and immutable ancestry prevents cycles. Children cannot begin before their parent's divergence. Archiving a parent preserves its descendants, their pins and their ability to author; archived parents cannot be used to create another branch.

`world_history_version` is append-only and snapshots the complete historical source, including dates, uncertainty, classifications, source reckoning and exact calendar context. Each version references its existing entry or era identity and the timeline that authored that interpretation. Referential columns protect source reckonings and anchored calendar versions in the same World. `world_history_version_era` preserves same-World historical relationships for each entry version. SQL triggers reject version and relationship updates/deletion.

`world_history_head` identifies a timeline's selected version for each historical identity, its independent edit revision, inheritance mode, direct-parent source/version/revision and optional extent of known predecessor existence. A branch copies these small references, not the World or historical payloads. Branch interpretations append versions against the same identity. They never update a parent's account or an existing descendant's pin. Historical archive/restore creates another local version and preserves provenance.

Primary History retains the established tables and editing contract. Each successful primary save/archive/restore captures a source version in the same transaction. Migration 0112 backfills versions/heads for existing records; branching also captures any primary records inserted directly by an older fixture. No author operation loads the entire revision archive into the browser.

## Divergence and uncertainty

The boundary is **the start of canonical Year N**. Fully dated records ending strictly before N are automatically inherited. Accounts beginning at N or later are not automatically inherited. Known windows and durations ending before N retain their distinct semantics. Crossing intervals, undated accounts and earlier planned accounts remain unresolved. Approximation has no authored error tolerance, so an approximate center before N cannot prove predecessor existence and is unresolved. Classifications are retained independently; inheritance does not convert disputed or disproven accounts to established truth.

An era known to begin before N and ending at/after N, or with an unknown ending, retains its complete immutable source and receives a partial effective projection. That projection has an unknown alternate ending, with an explicit statement of the year through which predecessor existence is known. No source date is changed. Unknown beginnings require a decision. A descendant of a partial era cannot automatically extend knowledge beyond its parent's pinned extent.

The source review keeps unresolved and excluded records outside effective History. The author may explicitly include a complete source, exclude it, or write a branch interpretation of an included source. Including a complete account requires confirmation that its uncertain or later portions are deliberately adopted. A branch interpretation can change dates, accuracy, narrative and era associations while retaining identity/provenance. Source era relationships outside the effective branch remain retained relationships, rather than an implicit adoption of that era.

If a direct parent's selected revision changes, source review marks the newer version. Explicit acceptance compares both the branch revision and the exact parent revision/version under the World lock, selects the new pin and reassesses divergence eligibility. Existing descendants keep their own versions. Concurrent or unchanged stale submissions fail without a write. New parent records added after branching are not automatically attached: this pass reconciles existing pinned identities, not newly introduced sources or history rebasing.

## Interface and navigation

The existing workspace adds a Timeline Manager and a selected-history control. Primary-only Worlds retain the normal History editor, timeline and chronological list. The manager shows parent relationships, divergence, authored explanations and lifecycle; every real History save carries the selected timeline. Editors identify that history and explain branch interpretations. Deep links use `?timeline=<stable-id>&tab=history|timelines`, preserving `review=1` as appropriate. Native history integrates with the installed Next router; internal router state is not copied into new entries. Unsaved forms retain their drafts on failure, offer explicit conflict reload and guard dismissal/navigation. Phone checks use a real touch-enabled browser context.

Calendars, immutable rules, origins, anchors, preferred notation and reckonings remain World definitions. Alternative browsing does not imply that a Primary History calendar adoption/reform occurred in that alternative: its calendar pane exposes shared definitions while clearly deferring timeline-specific adoption/reform authoring. Precise source dates remain usable and pinned; no canonical year is inferred from elapsed-day notation. No Campaign clock is read or changed.

## Bounds and future compatibility

The initial supported bounds are 256 retained timelines per World and 5,000 retained historical identities per timeline, including excluded and archived identities. Queries are limited and join only the selected versions; source review and chronological list render incrementally, while the axis retains its existing visible-group limits. Creation and individual editing reject writes beyond the supported bounds. Large individual accounts can increase projection size; pagination beyond these declared limits is future work.

Future World entities should retain one World identity and use `(world_id, timeline_id, entity_id)` state/version references, following the same immutable-source, pinned-inheritance and explicit-reconciliation principles. Atlas remains shared World geography in Bridge A; no alternate maps or automatic geographical divergence is claimed. Phase 4 entity editors, Campaign associations, VTT, NPC generation, gameplay effects, public sharing and System Canon controls are absent. Bridge B can reference stable World/timeline IDs and separately authored Campaign dates without replacing this architecture.

## Deployment and recovery

0112 follows 0111, with all pending predecessors applied in committed journal order. It creates four tables, adds/backfills the two origin references, snapshots existing history and installs protective triggers/indexes. No historical or Atlas payload is rewritten. Existing SQL and snapshots through 0111 are immutable. No environment variable, dependency or external service is required. The full rehearsal compares populated 0111 record projections, then checks reapplication and a fresh installation.

Stage the release/build, verify the target and migration ledger, pause authoring and protect a verified database backup. Apply committed migrations through the existing controlled migration process before activating this application. Smoke-test primary history, branch authoring, explicit review and private role access. This procedure requires separate deployment authorization.

A code revert does not undo migration 0112, its triggers, timeline identities or saved versions. An older release queries all World records without origin filtering and could blend branch-authored accounts into primary history; its writers also omit source-version capture. **Do not reactivate older Worlds readers/writers after branches exist without a compatibility fix or temporarily disabling Worlds access.** Prefer a forward fix. For backup restoration, stop writers, preserve subsequent edits for reconciliation, restore into a verified target and align code plus migration ledger with that restored state. Never drop pinned history or delete ledger rows to imitate a rollback.
