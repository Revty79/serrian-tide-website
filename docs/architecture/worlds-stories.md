## Identity and authoring

Stories extend `world_lore_identity` with the `story` family and reuse immutable lore headers, selected timeline heads, revision/audit rules, ownership and explicit Administrator read-only review. A separate competing identity or adoption registry would duplicate these invariants, so Phase 6A adds three typed child tables: `world_story_version`, `world_story_context` and `world_story_source`. Existing shared sections, participants, geographic references, History citations and authored periods remain real saved references.

The Lore, Stories & Legends workspace accepts a name alone. Classification is free text with optional suggestions; myth, rumor or prophecy never establishes truth. Ten optional narrative fields preserve full story, classification, origin, authorship, narrator, audience, transmission, historical uncertainty, interpretations and original source context. Named passages use stable custom-section identities. Contradictory accounts can remain different identities or local timeline interpretations. The application neither adjudicates them nor creates events from narrative text.

Telling/recording dates use the common lore header. Alleged-event dates use the separate context table. Both support undated, known, approximate, uncertain windows and durations, independent chronology notation and exact optional calendar sources. A calendar day supplies no missing canonical year. Transmission periods preserve independent beginnings/endings; an unknown ending never establishes continuing circulation. Chronology and calendar arithmetic are unchanged.

Saved readers offer explicit retelling and contradictory-account actions. Those create a new draft with a retained source; only Save creates another identity. Earlier accounts are selected from active saved stories in the same World and timeline. Their immutable version, chosen source revision, original name, text, dates and authorship remain available after rename/archive. Existing pins may be kept; new pins require a current available source with the submitted revision. Source refresh keeps the parent draft. Removing and reselecting a stale new pin deliberately chooses the refreshed version. Arbitrary version reads require a pin in the owner's current selected story; they cannot be requested by UUID alone.

Real World-family connections allow authored roles and unresolved wording. Places and event/era citations reuse existing same-World validation. Inverse readers show stories attached to creations/places and accounts that retain a story. Linking an existing event and writing a new event remain explicit actions; a story date is never an automatic History event. Retained story readers show their saved narrative rather than injecting subsequently connected current milestones.

## Timeline and privacy

Story continuity always needs deliberate review on divergence. The alleged-event date does not establish that the story was told before divergence. Authors may include an exact parent source, exclude it, interpret it locally, accept a deliberately selected newer parent source or author an independent story. Parent versions remain immutable; later parent stories require explicit adoption. Archive/restore uses the established append-only source rules.

Ordinary knowledge remains a private preview for the authorized owner or explicit read-only Administrator, without new Campaign permissions. The server filters sources before titles, selectors, summaries, paging and inverse projections. A protected story field, passage, transmission period, alleged context, original-source connection, revealing participant, confidential place or source calendar withholds the whole story. Private author notes alone are stripped. Public profiles in earlier families retain their established independent-field privacy behavior.

`ordinaryWorldSources` traverses version dependencies. It considers retained original versions and their selected effective current versions separately, and propagates protection across story/relationship/History dependencies. Deduplicated recursive sets terminate cyclic references. An old protected original stays withheld after its current account becomes public; an independently protected current account suppresses older pins. Pending, excluded or missing selected heads do not fall back to Primary. Confidential Atlas descendants remain hidden. Original narrative metadata cannot release an unavailable source.
